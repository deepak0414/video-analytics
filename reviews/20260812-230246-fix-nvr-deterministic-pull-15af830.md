## Verdict: approve

The rewrite replaces the dHash verify-and-trim + ReferenceLibrary design with a padded fetch + PTS cut + duration sanity gate, and it is done carefully. What I verified and it held up:

- **Two-phase `_pull_window` logic** (`src/va/sources/nvr.py:243-334`): phase iteration, per-phase retries, `break` on `raw is None` (correct — `_fetch_window` retries `MAX_TRIES` internally), atomic `os.replace`, temp-dir cleanup plus `_stop_load` in `finally`, and `_conn()` fail-fast before work. I recomputed the ring-edge arithmetic by hand: a head shortfall ≤ 2 s shifts the clip at full duration, 2–4 s passes the gate short-and-shifted, > 4 s fails through to the exact-window fallback — the documented "up to ~PAD_POST+DURATION_TOL_S" bound is exact.
- **Determinism rule compliance**: the deterministic claim is backed by reported ground truth (7/7 windows across lighting modes, byte-identical repeat pulls, a measured 20.0 s cut), and the removal of perceptual verification is justified by a concrete false-refusal incident (11 dusk windows). The pad constants are structural budgets, explicitly flagged and measurement-justified in comments — compliant with the hardcoded-content rule.
- **Test integrity**: all 20 deleted tests covered the deleted dHash/ReferenceLibrary subsystem; the orthogonal truncated-download test was preserved; the new flow is pinned (padded bounds, cut offsets, retry, fallback engagement with its `(0.0, 30.0)` cut, both-phases-exhausted raise, atomic landing, real-clip `_probe_cut` validation). I traced `_det_harness`'s probe sequencing against the code's actual call counts and the assertions match. `tests/test_watch.py:43` already doubles the new 4-arg `_pull_window` signature.
- **Contract/docs**: the signature change and wholesale symbol deletions are logged in COORDINATION.md; CLAUDE.md's `nvr://` and `va watch` blocks were rewritten in the same change and match the code; no dangling code references to deleted symbols; no new env vars/flags. The `need_agent_review:` subject is exempt from the clarity rule.

Two minor findings survive:

1. **Duration gate is vacuous for tiny windows** (`nvr.py:307`). The tolerance is absolute (±2.0 s), so for a window ≤ 2 s *any* decodable cut passes regardless of duration — and the production watcher can emit exactly such windows (`watch.py:69-86` floor/ceils an instant motion event into a 1 s window). A truncated or lead-in-only pull of a 1 s window — the exact-window fallback path, where the §5d lead-in is not padded away — ingests one frame of potentially foreign footage as a "clean" clip, contradicting the fail-closed claim. Safe path: scale the tolerance, e.g. `min(DURATION_TOL_S, 0.5 * window_len)`, or require `dur >= window_len - DURATION_TOL_S` with a fraction floor.
2. **Carried from the round-2 review, unaddressed (no dispute recorded)**: a ring-edge padded pull with a 0–4 s head shortfall passes the gate as a clip silently shifted late by up to ~4 s against `videos.start_epoch`, so the aligned exact-window fallback never engages for the case it exists to fix. It is documented in three places as a backlog caveat, but it is detectable today: probe the raw padded `.dav` against the requested padded span and, on a head shortfall, prefer the exact-window phase or correct the cut offset.

Verified and dissolved (not reported): the nested `MAX_TRIES` loops don't multiply on fetch failure (the phase breaks); `_probe_cut`'s `time=N/A` and missing-header cases fall through to `None`, which fails safe into a retry; retrying a byte-identical deterministic pull within a phase is wasted work but harmless; the misleadingly cut-focused final error message when the device simply served no data is preceded by per-phase warnings carrying the real failure strings.
